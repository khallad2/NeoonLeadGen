# Neon AI - Ideal Customer Profiles (ICP)

**Value Proposition:** We turn text, documentation, and silent footage into high-retention, avatar-led video content with motion graphics. 24-hour turnaround. Zero on-camera time required.
**Target Pricing:** $1,500 - $2,500 / month (Productized Retainer)

---

## ICP 1: The Stretched "DevRel" Team (Primary Target)

**Company Profile:**
*   **Type:** B2B Tech & SaaS companies (e.g., Developer Tools, API platforms, Cloud infrastructure).
*   **Stage:** Seed to Series B funded (they have deployed marketing budgets but small teams).
*   **Headcount:** 11 - 200 employees.

**Target Personas (Decision Makers):**
*   Head of Developer Relations (DevRel)
*   Developer Advocate
*   VP of Developer Marketing
*   CTO or Technical Founder (in smaller startups)

**The Core Pain Points:**
*   They produce excellent written documentation, release notes, and technical blogs, but struggle to convert them into YouTube or short-form videos.
*   Their engineers and advocates hate getting on camera or setting up lighting/audio.
*   Traditional video agencies don't understand their highly technical jargon or architectures.
*   Internal video editing creates a massive bottleneck.

**Buying Signals (Where to find them & what to look for):**
*   **LinkedIn:** Search for companies that just raised a funding round. Look at their company page—if they post links to their blog but have zero video content, they are a prime target.
*   **X (Twitter):** Search for DevRel advocates posting long text threads explaining technical concepts with static images, but no videos.

---

## ICP 2: The "Silent" Tech Founder 

**Company Profile:**
*   **Type:** Bootstrapped SaaS, specialized dev agencies, or profitable micro-SaaS.
*   **Revenue:** $10k+ MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue).

**Target Personas:**
*   Solo Founder / Co-Founder
*   Indie Hacker
*   Agency Owner

**The Core Pain Points:**
*   They know personal branding on X, LinkedIn, and YouTube is the best way to get inbound users, but they are terrified of the camera or think their English speaking skills aren't "YouTube ready."
*   They are too busy writing code and doing sales to learn Premiere Pro or CapCut.
*   They want to "build in public" but only have time to write tweets.

**Buying Signals (Where to find them & what to look for):**
*   **X (Twitter):** Search the `#buildinpublic` hashtag. Look for founders who are launching products with text and screenshots only. 
*   **ProductHunt:** Look at the top products of the day. If a tech product has a great UI but a terrible, unedited screen-recorded Loom video as their explainer, they need your service.

---

## ICP 3: The High-Ticket Technical Educator

**Company Profile:**
*   **Type:** E-learning, bootcamps, and high-ticket cohort-based courses.
*   **Product Price:** Selling courses or memberships for $500+.

**Target Personas:**
*   Course Creator
*   Technical Instructor
*   Bootcamp Founder

**The Core Pain Points:**
*   They need highly accurate motion graphics and b-roll to explain complex coding concepts, system designs, or architectures. 
*   Updating old course materials takes weeks of re-filming.
*   They want to use short-form video (TikTok/Reels) for top-of-funnel lead generation but don't have the bandwidth.

**Buying Signals (Where to find them & what to look for):**
*   **YouTube:** Search for coding/tech tutorial channels with 10k - 50k subscribers. If their videos are just basic screen shares with a tiny bubble of their face in the corner and no engaging graphics, they are losing retention and need your pipeline.

---

## The "Anti-ICP" (Who to Disqualify & Avoid)

Do not waste time scraping or emailing these groups, as they will drain your time or reject the AI avatar concept:
1.  **Low-Budget Beginners:** Anyone who asks "Can we do a trial for $100?" (They don't value the infrastructure).
2.  **Vloggers & Lifestyle Creators:** Their audience follows them for their authentic, human personality and behind-the-scenes life. An AI avatar will ruin their brand.
3.  **E-commerce Physical Product Brands:** Your motion graphics and avatar pipeline are built for explaining digital concepts, software, and tech—not modeling physical clothes or makeup.
